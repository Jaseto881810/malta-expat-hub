  - heading: Who sends the bill?
    text: |-
      In Malta, electricity and water come on **one bill** from **ARMS Ltd** (Automated Revenue Management Services).

      - **Enemalta** supplies the electricity and the **Water Services Corporation** supplies the water.
      - ARMS reads the **smart meters**, sends the bills and collects the money.
      - Bills usually arrive **every two months**. You can see your bills, readings and payments in the **ARMS online account** and pay online, by direct debit or at a post office.

      Electricity in Malta is **cheap compared with most of Europe**, but bills can go up a lot in **summer**, when people use air conditioning.

  - heading: If you rent - bills in the landlord's name
    text: |-
      When you rent, the ARMS account is **often kept in the landlord's name**. This is normal and legal. In that case:

      - ARMS sends the bill **to the landlord**.
      - The landlord then **passes the bill amount on to you**, the tenant. You pay the landlord, usually every two months or with your rent.
      - Some landlords ask for a **fixed amount every month** and settle the difference later.

      **What to check:**

      - **Ask for a copy of each ARMS bill**, so you can see the real meter readings and the amount. You should only pay for what you used, at the price ARMS charged.
      - **Check the tariff on the bill:** it should say **Residential**, with the right **number of persons**. If it says *Domestic* or *Non-residential*, you are probably paying too much (see below).
      - **Write the meter readings** on the day you move in and the day you move out. Take a photo of the meters.
      - **Read your lease:** it should say how bills are shared and how often you pay.

      You can also ask to move the account into **your own name**. This gives you full control, but there is an admin fee and the landlord must agree.

  - heading: Residential or domestic tariff - why it matters
    text: |-
      ARMS uses different prices depending on who lives in the home:

      | Tariff | For | Price |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | **Residential** | Your main home, with the people living there registered | **Cheapest**, plus an *eco-reduction* discount if you use little electricity |
      | **Domestic** | Homes where nobody is registered as living there, such as second homes | Higher |
      | **Non-residential** | Businesses, holiday lets, empty property | Highest |

      **Example (2026 electricity prices):** the first 2,000 units a year cost about **€0.105 per unit** on the residential tariff, and about **€0.14** on the domestic tariff. Water costs about **€1.40 per m³** (residential) or **€2.56 per m³** (domestic). Check the latest prices on the ARMS website.

      Many expats pay the **domestic** price for months without knowing, because nobody registered them at the address.

  - heading: How to get the residential tariff
    text: |-
      1. **Get your e-Residence card** (or Maltese ID card). See [Residence Cards](/work-business/residence-cards.html).
      2. **Fill in Form H** (*Declaration of Number of Persons*). It tells ARMS how many people live in the home. Attach a copy of the ID card or residence card of **everyone aged 16 or over**.
      3. **Send it online** through servizz.gov.mt or the ARMS website. It takes about **3 weeks** to process.
      4. **Check your next bill.** It should show *Residential* and the right number of persons.

      **Good to know:**

      - Each person can be registered at **one address only**. When you move, submit a new Form H for the new home.
      - Often the **landlord or agent** submits Form H, because the account is in their name. Ask them to do it when you sign the lease.
      - If the landlord **refuses to help**, a tenant with a signed lease can apply to ARMS directly with **Form F2**, so the tenant is recognised at the address.
      - The new tariff applies to **bills issued after** ARMS processes the form, so do it as soon as you move in.

  - heading: How much will I pay?
    text: |-
      It depends on the size of the home, how many people live there and how much you use air conditioning. As a rough guide for an apartment on the residential tariff:

      - **1 to 2 people:** about **€60 to €120 a month**
      - **Family of 3 to 4:** about **€100 to €180 a month**
      - **Summer** bills (June to September) can be **double** the winter bills.

      Use the **ARMS bill calculator** on the ARMS website to check a bill, or plan your monthly budget with our free [Cost of Living Calculator](/tools/cost-of-living.html).

  - heading: Tips to save money
    text: |-
      - **Make sure you are on the residential tariff.** This is the biggest saving.
      - **Use air conditioning wisely:** set it to 24–26°C in summer, close windows and shutters, and clean the filters.
      - **Check the solar water heater** if your home has one, and switch off the electric water heater when you do not need it.
      - **Fix dripping taps and toilets.** A running cistern can waste a lot of water.
      - **Compare your meter readings** between bills to spot problems early.
      - Many people in Malta **do not drink tap water**. See [Water Delivery](/services/water-delivery.html) for bottled water options.

  - heading: Good to know
    text: |-
      - **Moving in:** ask the landlord or agent which tariff the home is on before you sign, and how bills are paid.
      - **Moving out:** give the final meter readings to the landlord and keep a photo. Ask for the final bill before your deposit is returned.
      - **Problems with your landlord?** See [Renting Properties](/accommodation/renting.html) and [Housing Authority](/accommodation/authority.html).

      > Prices and forms can change. Always check the latest information with ARMS before you make a decision.
