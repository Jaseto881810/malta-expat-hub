  - heading: The basic rules
    text: |-
      Malta follows the **EU pet travel rules**. If you follow them, there is **no quarantine**. These rules are for **dogs, cats and ferrets** travelling with their owner (up to **5 pets**).

      Every pet needs:

      1. **A microchip** (15-digit ISO standard). It must be put in **before** the rabies vaccine.
      2. **A rabies vaccine**, given when the pet is at least 12 weeks old. You must wait **21 days** after the first vaccine before travelling.
      3. **A pet passport or health certificate** (see below).

      **Dogs only:** Malta is free of a dangerous tapeworm, so every dog needs a **tapeworm treatment** given by a vet **1 to 5 days (24 to 120 hours) before arriving** in Malta. The vet writes the date and time in the passport or certificate.

      > If any document is missing or wrong, your pet can be sent back or put in quarantine at your cost. Check everything with your vet before you travel.

  - heading: Rules by country
    text: |-
      | Coming from | Documents | Extra steps |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | **EU country** | **EU pet passport** | Microchip, rabies vaccine, and tapeworm treatment for dogs |
      | **UK and other "listed" countries** (for example the USA, Canada, Australia, Japan) | **EU animal health certificate**, signed by an official vet within 10 days before travel | Same as EU, no blood test needed |
      | **Other non-EU countries** | **EU animal health certificate** | A **rabies blood test** (titre test) at least 30 days after the vaccine, then a **3-month wait** before travel |

      **Young pets:** puppies and kittens must be at least **15 weeks old** to come from the EU or a listed country, and about **7 months old** from other countries.

      Rules were updated in 2026. Always check the latest rules for your country with the **Veterinary Regulation Directorate (VRD)** in Malta and your vet.

  - heading: Travelling to Malta
    text: |-
      - **Tell the VRD before you arrive.** Malta asks for **advance notice** of pets arriving, usually at least **48 hours** before. Find the form and contact details on the VRD website.
      - **By air:** pets from outside the EU must arrive at **Malta International Airport**. Airlines have their own rules: small pets can sometimes travel in the cabin, bigger pets go in the hold or as cargo, in an approved crate. Book early, because places for pets are limited.
      - **By ferry from Sicily:** check the ferry company's pet rules. Pets usually stay in your car or in a special area.
      - **Hot weather:** many airlines do not carry pets in the hold in the hottest summer months. Plan your move for spring or autumn if you can.
    html: ''
    custom: ''
  - heading: After you arrive
    text: |-
      - **Register your dog in Malta.** Dogs in Malta must be **microchipped and registered** on the national database. Ask a **local vet** to register your dog's microchip soon after you arrive. A small fee applies.
      - **Find a vet.** There are many vet clinics across Malta and Gozo, and some offer 24-hour emergency care.
      - **Protect against local diseases.** Ask your vet about **leishmaniasis** (spread by sandflies in warm months) and **ticks**. A special collar or vaccine is common for dogs in Malta.
      - **Think about pet insurance.** Vet bills can be high for surgery or emergencies.

  - heading: Renting with a pet
    text: |-
      Finding a flat that accepts pets can be **hard**, especially in Sliema, St Julian's and Gżira.

      - **Say you have a pet from the start**, and ask the agent to search only for pet-friendly homes.
      - The landlord may ask for a **higher deposit**.
      - Make sure the **lease says pets are allowed**, so there are no problems later.
      - Homes with a yard or terrace, or in quieter towns and villages, are often easier. See [Renting Properties](/accommodation/renting.html).

  - heading: Daily life with a pet in Malta
    text: |-
      - **Heat:** summer pavements get very hot. Walk dogs early in the morning or late in the evening, and **never leave a pet in a car**.
      - **Beaches:** dogs are not allowed on most popular beaches in the bathing season. Malta and Gozo have **dog-friendly beaches**, for example in Mellieħa, Sliema, Kalkara, Marsaskala, Marsaxlokk and Birżebbuġa. Keep your dog on a leash and pick up after it.
      - **Leash and waste:** keep dogs on a leash in public places and always clean up. Fines apply.
      - **Green spaces:** Malta has few large parks, but places like **Ta' Qali National Park** and the countryside are good for walks in the cooler months.
      - **Adopting:** many dogs and cats need homes. Contact the **Animal Welfare** centre at Ta' Qali or local animal charities.
    html: ''
    custom: ''
  - heading: Good to know
    text: |-
      - Malta has **no banned dog breeds**, but some hybrid animals (such as wolf hybrids and some wild-type cats) cannot be imported.
      - For other pets, such as **birds, rabbits or reptiles**, different rules apply. Ask the VRD before you travel.
      - Moving with a pet adds costs: vet visits, certificates, flights and maybe a pet relocation company. Plan your budget with our [Cost of Living Calculator](/tools/cost-of-living.html).

      > Pet travel rules can change. Always check the latest rules with the Veterinary Regulation Directorate (VRD) and your vet before you travel.
