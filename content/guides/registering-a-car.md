  - heading: Driving in Malta - the basics
    text: |-
      - **Malta drives on the LEFT**, like the UK and Ireland. Cars have the **steering wheel on the right**.
      - **Overtake on the right.** At **roundabouts**, go **clockwise** and give way to traffic coming from the right.
      - **Speed limits:** usually **50 km/h** in towns and **60–80 km/h** on main roads. There are no motorways.
      - **Traffic** is heavy at rush hour (about 7:30–9:00 and 16:30–18:30), especially around Msida, Sliema and St Julian's.
      - **Parking** can be hard in busy areas. White lines are free, blue lines are paid, yellow lines mean no parking, and green lines are for residents with a permit.

      If you come from a country that drives on the right, take it slowly at first, especially at roundabouts and when turning.

  - heading: Do you need a car?
    text: |-
      Many expats in central areas live **without a car**. Buses are free for many residents, and Bolt, Uber and eCabs are cheap. See [Public Transport](/quick-guides/public-transport.html).

      A car is more useful if you live in a village, in the north or south, or in Gozo, or if you have children. Compare the total cost below with taxis and buses before you decide. Our [Cost of Living Calculator](/tools/cost-of-living.html) can help.

  - heading: Bringing your car - the main rules
    text: |-
      If you **move to Malta** with a car registered abroad, you must **register it in Malta within 30 days** of arriving. Late registration can cost **€30 for each day** late.

      Registration is done with **Transport Malta**. You usually pay:

      - **Registration tax:** a one-time tax based on the car's **value**, its **CO2 emissions** and its **length**. Cleaner and smaller cars pay less. Old and high-emission cars (especially older diesels) pay much more.
      - **Small fees:** about **€15** for registration and **€70** for number plates.
      - **VAT or customs duty**, in some cases (see below).

      **Electric cars** and plug-in hybrids (with at least 50 km of electric range) pay **no registration tax**.

      Tip: use the **Transport Malta valuation tool** or an online import calculator before you ship your car, to see how much tax you will pay.

  - heading: From the EU, the UK or other countries
    text: |-
      | Coming from | VAT and duty | Roadworthiness test |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | **EU country, used car** (over 6 months old **and** over 6,000 km) | No VAT, no duty | Valid EU test certificates are accepted |
      | **EU country, new car** (6 months or less, **or** 6,000 km or less) | **18% Maltese VAT** | Not needed |
      | **UK** | Usually **10% duty + 18% VAT** for used cars (some cars may pay no duty) | Needed for older cars |
      | **Other non-EU countries** | **10% duty + 18% VAT** on the price plus shipping | Needed for cars over 4 years old |

      Cars from outside the EU also need a **customs declaration** (usually done by a customs broker) and a **€55 inspection**.

      **Moving your home to Malta? You may pay no registration tax.** With a *Transfer of Residence* application, you can bring **one car** without registration tax if you **owned it for at least 24 months** and **lived abroad for at least 24 months** before moving. Apply within 30 days. If you sell the car in Malta later, the tax becomes due.

  - heading: Importing step by step
    text: |-
      1. **Check the costs** with an import calculator or Transport Malta, before you decide.
      2. **Get your documents:** registration certificate, purchase invoice, Certificate of Conformity (CoC), proof of insurance and your ID or e-Residence card. From outside the EU, also the export or de-registration certificate.
      3. **Ship the car.** Most cars come by **ro-ro ferry** from Italy (for example Genoa or Salerno). Shipping and port costs are often **€200–600** or more.
      4. **Clear customs** (non-EU cars only), with a customs broker.
      5. **Get a roadworthiness test (VRT)** if needed.
      6. **Register the car with Transport Malta**, pay the registration tax and get **Maltese number plates**.
      7. **Insure the car** and pay the **road licence**.

  - heading: Buying a car in Malta
    text: |-
      Often the easier choice is to **buy a car in Malta**. The registration tax is already paid.

      - **Used cars:** look on **Maltapark**, local dealers and Facebook groups. Dealers are often more expensive but may give a warranty.
      - **Check before you buy:** ask for the **VRT certificate** and service history, and think about a check by a mechanic.
      - **Transfer of ownership:** the buyer and seller complete the transfer at **Transport Malta** (or online), so the car is in your name.
      - **New electric cars:** there are government **grants** for new electric cars. Check the current scheme on the Transport Malta website.

      Remember that cars in Malta are often **more expensive** than in many other EU countries, because of the registration tax.

      | Cost | About how much |
      | --- | --- |
      | **Road licence** (yearly tax) | €100–525 for a newer petrol car, more for older cars. €0 for electric cars in the first 5 years |
      | **Insurance** (required) | €200–400 third party, €400–800 comprehensive |
      | **Roadworthiness test (VRT)** | At 4 years old, then every 2 years. Up to about €30 |
      | **Fuel** | About €1.41 per litre petrol, €1.24 diesel (fixed prices in 2026) |
      | **Parking** | Residents' permit about €50 a year. Paid parking and the Valletta access charge extra |

      > Car taxes can change with each national budget. The Budget for 2027 may bring changes, so always check the latest rates with Transport Malta before you import or buy.
