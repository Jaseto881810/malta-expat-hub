  - heading: Can I be self-employed in Malta?
    text: |-
      - **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens:** yes. You can register as self-employed in the same way as Maltese citizens.
      - **Non-EU citizens:** you need a permit that allows self-employment. This is not easy to get. Ask Identità or a licensed adviser before you start.

      You can be **full-time** self-employed, or **part-time** self-employed next to a normal job.

  - heading: Step by step
    text: |-
      1. **Get your ID and tax number.** Your e-Residence card number is also your tax number. See [Residence Cards](/work-business/residence-cards.html).
      2. **Register with Jobsplus as self-employed.** Fill in the Jobsplus self-employment form (full-time or part-time) and email it to Jobsplus.
      3. **Register for VAT.** Apply online with the Malta Tax and Customs Administration (MTCA). Choose Article 10 or Article 11 (see below).
      4. **Open a bank account** for your business. Keep business and personal money separate.
      5. **Start invoicing.** Give your clients a VAT invoice or fiscal receipt for every sale.
      6. **Pay your social security (Class 2) and tax.** See the guides below.
      7. **Keep your records.** Keep all invoices and receipts for at least **10 years**.

      Some jobs also need a licence or permit, for example food, building or financial services. **Business First**, the government's business help desk, can tell you if you need one.

    custom: ''
  - heading: VAT - Article 10 or Article 11?
    text: |-
      | | Article 11 (small business) | Article 10 (standard) |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | **Who** | Turnover up to **€35,000** a year | Turnover above €35,000, or you choose it |
      | **Charge VAT to clients?** | No | Yes, usually **18%** |
      | **Claim back VAT you pay?** | No | Yes |
      | **VAT number** | No "MT" in front | Starts with "MT" |
      | **Returns** | One declaration a year | A VAT return every 3 months |

      Most new freelancers with local clients start with **Article 11**. If you work for businesses in other EU countries, or buy services from abroad, you may also need an **Article 12** registration. Ask an accountant.
    html: ''
    custom: ''
  - heading: Tax and social security
    text: |-
      - **Income tax:** you pay tax on your **profit** (income minus business costs), at the normal rates. Part-time self-employed people may be able to pay a flat 15% on the first €12,000 of profit. Read [Income Tax for Self-Employed and Businesses](/work-business/business-tax.html).
      - **Social security:** you pay **Class 2** contributions, based on your profit. Read [Social Security for the Self-Employed](/work-business/self-employed-social-security.html).
      - **When to pay:** tax and social security are paid in three parts during the year. Read [Payment Dates for VAT, Tax and Social Security](/work-business/tax-payment-dates.html).
    html: ''
    custom: ''
  - heading: Good to know
    text: |-
      > **Self-employed or employee?** If you work full-time for one client, at their office, with their tools and their hours, Malta may see you as an **employee**, not self-employed. Jobsplus checks this with a set of rules. Read more in [Income Tax for Self-Employed and Businesses](/work-business/business-tax.html).

      A local accountant usually costs a few hundred euro a year for a simple sole trader. It is often worth it in your first year.
