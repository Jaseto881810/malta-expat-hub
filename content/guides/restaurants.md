- heading: The Maltese Dining Scene
  text: |
    Dining in Malta is a deeply Mediterranean experience, characterized by long, social meals, an abundance of fresh local produce, and a heavy Italian and Sicilian influence. Because of its location in the center of the Mediterranean and its booming expat population, the island's restaurant scene has evolved rapidly into a diverse, high-quality culinary destination, recently gaining significant recognition from the Michelin Guide.

    While you can find everything from authentic Japanese sushi to high-end Argentine steakhouses in the central hubs, stepping out into the traditional villages offers a deeply rustic, comforting local culinary experience.
- heading: 'Traditional Maltese Food: What is it?'
  html: |
    <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
    <p>Traditional Maltese cuisine is hearty, slow-cooked, and built around what historically could be farmed locally or pulled from the sea.</p>
    <ul class="list-none space-y-4 mt-4">
    <li class="p-4 bg-slate-50 border border-slate-100 rounded-xl">
    <b class="text-[#003366] text-lg">Stuffat tal-Fenek (Rabbit Stew)</b>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1">This is the national dish. Rabbit is marinated and slow-cooked in a rich red wine, garlic, and tomato sauce until it falls off the bone. A traditional "Fenkata" meal starts with a massive bowl of spaghetti tossed in the rabbit sauce, followed by the meat served with thick, roasted potatoes.</p>
    </li>
    <li class="p-4 bg-slate-50 border border-slate-100 rounded-xl">
    <b class="text-[#003366] text-lg">Braġioli (Beef Olives)</b>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1">A highly popular main course. Thin slices of beef are rolled around a stuffing of minced meat, bacon, garlic, parsley, and hard-boiled egg, then slow-braised in a rich wine and tomato sauce. (Despite the English name, there are no actual olives in the dish).</p>
    </li>
    <li class="p-4 bg-slate-50 border border-slate-100 rounded-xl">
    <b class="text-[#003366] text-lg">Aljotta (Fish Soup)</b>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1">A garlicky, aromatic fish broth heavily seasoned with fresh mint, marjoram, and tomatoes. It usually features small chunks of local rockfish and rice.</p>
    </li>
    <li class="p-4 bg-slate-50 border border-slate-100 rounded-xl">
    <b class="text-[#003366] text-lg">Lampuki Pie</b>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1">Lampuki (Dolphin Fish/Mahi-Mahi) is the most famous local catch, migrating past Malta in late summer. It is traditionally baked into a savory pie filled with spinach, cauliflower, chestnuts, and sultanas.</p>
    </li>
    <li class="p-4 bg-slate-50 border border-slate-100 rounded-xl">
    <b class="text-[#003366] text-lg">Snacks: Pastizzi &amp; Ftira</b>
    <p class="text-sm mt-1">If you are on the go, grab a <i>Pastizz</i> (a flaky, diamond-shaped pastry filled with ricotta or mushy peas) or a <i>Ftira</i> (a crusty, ring-shaped bread stuffed with tuna, capers, olives, and tomato paste) from any local bakery.</p>
    </li>
    </ul>
    </div>
- heading: Where to Find Traditional Maltese Food
  html: |
    <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
    <p>To experience the best traditional food, you generally need to leave the commercial centers and visit the rural villages or fishing towns.</p>
    <div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-4 mt-4">
    <div class="border border-red-200 rounded-xl p-5 bg-red-50">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-red-800 text-lg mb-2">Mġarr (For Rabbit)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm mb-2">The small agricultural village of Mġarr in the northwest is the undisputed capital of the <b>Fenkata</b>. The town square is lined with highly traditional, rustic restaurants (like <i>Il-Barri</i> or <i>United Restaurant</i>) that serve the best rabbit and horse meat stew on the island. Portions are massive, and booking on a weekend is essential.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="border border-blue-200 rounded-xl p-5 bg-blue-50">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-blue-800 text-lg mb-2">Marsaxlokk (For Seafood)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm mb-2">This picturesque fishing village in the south is famous for its Sunday fish market. The entire waterfront is packed with excellent seafood restaurants (such as <i>Tartarun</i> or <i>La Nostra Padrona</i>) serving whatever the colorful Luzzu boats caught that morning.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="border border-amber-200 rounded-xl p-5 bg-amber-50">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-amber-800 text-lg mb-2">Valletta (Upscale Maltese)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm mb-2">If you want traditional Maltese flavors presented with modern, fine-dining flair, Valletta is the place to be. Restaurants like <i>Noni</i> (Michelin Star) or <i>Rubino</i> offer elevated, highly sophisticated takes on classic local ingredients.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="border border-emerald-200 rounded-xl p-5 bg-emerald-50">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-emerald-800 text-lg mb-2">Gozo (Rustic Charm)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm mb-2">Malta's sister island is renowned for its farm-to-table approach. Restaurants in Xagħra and Victoria serve excellent local sheep's cheese (Ġbejna), sun-dried tomatoes, and traditional Gozitan Ftira (a wood-fired, open-faced pizza).</p>
    </div>
    </div>
    </div>
- heading: Global Cuisine & Fine Dining
  text: |
    Because of the massive expatriate workforce in the iGaming and finance sectors, **Sliema and St. Julian's** have developed into incredible international culinary hubs. In these towns, you can find world-class sushi, authentic regional Indian cuisine, high-end South American steakhouses, and incredible Lebanese street food.

    **Italian Dominance:** Due to proximity, Malta has a massive Italian expat community. Consequently, the quality of pizza, pasta, and regional Italian dining across the island is exceptional, often rivaling what you would find in Rome or Naples.

- heading: Dining Etiquette & General Costs
  text: |
    - **Reservations:** The Maltese love to eat out, particularly on Friday nights and Sunday afternoons. **Always book in advance.** If you try to walk into a popular restaurant in Marsaxlokk or Valletta on a weekend without a reservation, you will likely be turned away.
    - **Tipping:** A service charge is rarely included on the bill. It is customary and expected to leave a tip of **5% to 10%** if the service was good, usually left in cash on the table.
    - **General Costs (2026):** A standard main course (pasta or pizza) at a mid-range restaurant usually costs between €12 and €18. A high-quality fish or steak main will range from €25 to €40. A casual dinner for two with local wine will typically cost around €60 - €80.
    - **Dining Times:** Maltese dining times align with Southern Europe. Dinner reservations usually start from 7:30 PM onwards, with restaurants getting busiest around 8:30 PM or 9:00 PM.
