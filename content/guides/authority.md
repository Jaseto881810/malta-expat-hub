- heading: What is the Malta Housing Authority?
  text: |
    The **Housing Authority** is a government entity tasked with overseeing the housing market in Malta. While historically focused on social housing and affordable housing schemes, its role completely transformed with the introduction of the **Private Residential Leases Act (2020)**.

    Today, the Housing Authority regulates the entire private rental market. Their primary goal is to ensure transparency, prevent tax evasion, and protect both landlords and tenants from abusive practices. **By law, every single private rental contract in Malta must be registered with the Housing Authority online.** An unregistered rental contract is considered null, void, and entirely illegal.

    Visit the [Malta Housign Authority](https://housingauthority.gov.mt/) website.
- heading: What is Rent Registration?
  text: |
    **Rent Registration** deals only with private residential leases, meaning ordinary landlord–tenant contracts between private parties. On the Housing Authority’s own site, it’s described as the place to register your private rent contract with Housing Authority

    The Rent Registration site lets you create and terminate a registration, submit a dispute, access the Arbitration Panel, and find forms and publications, and it has a portal where landlords and tenants can log in or create an account.

    You’d go here if you’re a landlord or tenant who needs to register a new lease, end one, or raise a dispute about a private rental.

    If you’re renting privately in Malta, registering the contract is generally a legal requirement under the Private Residential Leases Act, and Rent Registration is where you’d do it.

    Visit the [Rent Registration](https://rentregistration.mt/) website.

    Need to file a dispute with a landlord? File it by [Clicking Here](https://rentregistration.mt/how-to-submit-a-dispute/).
- heading: Responsibilities of the Landlord
  text: |
    Under Maltese law, landlords are legally bound to several strict obligations when leasing their property:

    - **Contract Registration:** The landlord has 10 days from the commencement of the lease to register the contract online with the Housing Authority. If they fail to do so, the tenant can register it themselves at the landlord's expense.
    - **Inventory:** The landlord must provide a detailed inventory list (often with photographs) of the condition of the property and its contents at the start of the lease. Both parties must sign this.
    - **Structural Maintenance:** The landlord is strictly responsible for all major, structural, or extraordinary maintenance (e.g., a broken water heater, a leaking roof, or a faulty washing machine that broke due to age, not tenant negligence).
    - **Notice Period:** For a standard 1-year contract, the landlord must give the tenant at least **3 months' written notice** if they do not intend to renew the contract. If no notice is given, the lease automatically renews for another year.
    - **Deposit Return:** The landlord must return the security deposit (minus any agreed deductions for damages or unpaid bills) promptly at the termination of the lease.
- heading: Responsibilities of the Tenant
  text: |
    Tenants also have strict legal and contractual obligations to maintain the property and respect the lease terms:

    - **Rent and Utilities:** The tenant must pay the agreed rent and utility bills (water, electricity, internet) on time. Failure to pay rent for two consecutive months gives the landlord grounds for immediate legal eviction.
    - **Minor Maintenance:** While landlords handle major repairs, the tenant is responsible for "ordinary" or minor maintenance. This includes changing lightbulbs, fixing a blocked sink caused by usage, or replacing broken items due to negligence.
    - **Access for Inspections:** The tenant must allow the landlord access to the property for inspections, provided the landlord gives reasonable advance notice (usually 24-48 hours). A landlord cannot enter unannounced.
    - **No Unauthorized Sub-Letting:** A tenant cannot sub-let the property or rent out a spare room (e.g., on Airbnb) without the explicit, written permission of the landlord.
    - **Notice Periods for Leaving:** A tenant cannot simply leave halfway through a contract. For a standard 1-year lease, the tenant can only terminate the contract \*after\* the first 6 months have passed, and they must give **1 month's notice** to the landlord and the Housing Authority via the official online portal.
- heading: How to Log a Complaint (Dispute Resolution)
  html: |
    <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
    <p>If a dispute arises—such as a landlord refusing to return a deposit, or a tenant refusing to pay for damages—the Housing Authority offers a streamlined legal process to avoid lengthy civil court battles.</p>
    <p>This is handled by the <b>Adjudicating Panel for Private Residential Leases</b>.</p>
    <ol class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-4 mt-4">
    <li><b>Attempt Amicable Resolution:</b> Before filing, it is highly recommended to communicate the issue clearly in writing (email or registered letter) to the other party, keeping all evidence.</li>
    <li><b>File a Claim Online:</b> If unresolved, either the landlord or the tenant can file a claim through the Housing Authority's online portal (<i>rentregistration.mt</i>). The claim must clearly state the issue and include all evidence (contracts, emails, photos, receipts).</li>
    <li><b>The Scope of the Panel:</b> The Panel only hears claims where the disputed amount is <b>up to €5,000</b>. (If a landlord is claiming €10,000 in damages, they must go through the standard civil courts).</li>
    <li><b>The Hearing:</b> The opposing party will be notified and given a chance to reply. The Adjudicating Panel will then summon both parties for a hearing.</li>
    <li><b>The Binding Decision:</b> The Panel will issue a ruling. This decision is legally binding and carries the same weight as a court judgment. It can only be appealed on points of law, not facts.</li>
    </ol>
    <div class="bg-blue-50 p-4 rounded-xl border border-blue-100 mt-4 text-sm text-blue-900">
    <b>Important Note on Evictions:</b> The Adjudicating Panel handles financial disputes. If a landlord needs to forcefully evict a tenant who refuses to leave after a contract ends or after unpaid rent, the landlord must file a separate eviction application before the Rent Regulation Board (a specific court of law). Landlords cannot legally change the locks or cut off utilities; doing so is a criminal offense.
