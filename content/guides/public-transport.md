  - heading: Getting around Malta
    text: |-
      Malta has **no trains or metro**. Most people travel by **bus**, **ferry**, **ride-hailing apps** or car.

      - **Buses** are run by Malta Public Transport and are called **tallinja**. They cover Malta and Gozo, including the airport.
      - **Ferries** connect Valletta with Sliema and the Three Cities, and Malta with Gozo.
      - **Bolt, Uber and eCabs** are cheap and popular, especially at night.

      Use the **tallinja app** or Google Maps to plan your trip and see live bus times.

  - heading: Free bus travel for residents
    text: |-
      Since October 2022, bus travel is **free for residents** who have a **personalised tallinja card**.

      - Free travel covers **day and night buses**, some special services, the **Valletta ferries** and the **Barrakka Lift** in Valletta.
      - **Not free:** the *tallinja Direct* express routes (TD), which take a fare from your card.
      - **Always tap your card** on the machine next to the driver. A green light means it worked. If you do not tap, you can get a **€50 fine**.

      > **Important:** free travel will change for many non-EU residents from **January 2027**. See below.

  - heading: Personalised tallinja card types
    text: |-
      | Card | Who it is for | Card fee |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | **Adult** | Residents aged 17 and over | €25 |
      | **Student** | Ages 11 to 16, or 17 to 59 in a full-time course of more than 3 months | €25 |
      | **Child** | Ages 4 to 10 (children under 4 travel free without a card) | €5 |
      | **Gozo Resident** | People whose main home is in Gozo | €25 |
      | **Concession** | People aged 60+ with a Kartanzjan card, and people with an EU Disability Card or CRPD ID card | €5 |

      **How to apply:**

      1. Register on the **Malta Public Transport website** (publictransport.com.mt) with your email and mobile number.
      2. Upload a **photo** and a copy of your **ID** (for most expats, your e-Residence card).
      3. Pay the card fee and postage. The card usually arrives **within 2 weeks**.

      You can also apply on paper at a **MaltaPost** post office, for a small extra fee.

  - heading: Cards and tickets for tourists and visitors
    text: |-
      Visitors and people without a personalised card pay for each trip, or buy a travel card:

      | Ticket or card | What you get | Price |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | **Single ticket** (cash or contactless card on the bus) | One trip, valid for 2 hours with changes | €2.00 in winter, €2.50 in summer, €3 at night |
      | **12 Single Day Journeys** | 12 trips, can be shared, valid 6 months | €19 |
      | **Explore Flex 4 Days** | Unlimited buses for 4 days, incl. night buses and the airport | from €21 |
      | **Explore Flex 7 Days** | Unlimited buses for 7 days | €27 |
      | **Explore Child 7 Day** | Unlimited buses for children aged 4 to 10 | €7 |

      **Explore Flex add-ons:** you can add a Gozo fast ferry return, a harbour cruise, a Comino trip or the hop-on hop-off bus. Prices are on the Malta Public Transport website.

      Explore cards cannot be shared. All cards work on buses in **both Malta and Gozo**. Prices are for 2026 and can change.

  - heading: What changes in 2027
    text: |-
      In the **Budget for 2027** (presented on 26 October 2026), the government announced that **free public transport will continue only for**:

      - **Maltese citizens**
      - **EU citizens living in Malta**
      - **Long-term residents** (a special status you can apply for, usually after 5 years of legal stay, with conditions)

      **Who will have to pay from January 2027:** most other **non-EU residents (third-country nationals)**, for example people on a Single Permit (including Key Employee Initiative), Nomad permit holders, and people on residence programmes such as the MPRP.

      **What we know so far:**

      - The new rules are expected to start in **January 2027**.
      - The **new fares and the exact rules have not been published yet**. Reports mention a **monthly pass of about €33**, but this is not yet confirmed.
      - The position of **EEA and Swiss citizens** and **British citizens** covered by the Brexit agreement still needs to be confirmed in the final rules.
      - The Budget also changes other things for non-EU residents, such as **higher work permit fees** and **free childcare** for new applicants.

      > We will update this guide as soon as the official rules and fares are published. If you are a non-EU resident, plan for a bus pass in your **2027 budget**. Try our [Cost of Living Calculator](/tools/cost-of-living.html).

  - heading: Ferries
    text: |-
      - **Valletta ferries:** short crossings from Valletta to **Sliema** and to the **Three Cities** (Birgu). Free with a personalised tallinja card.
      - **Gozo Channel ferry:** from **Ċirkewwa** (north Malta) to **Mġarr** (Gozo), about 25 minutes. Foot passengers and cars pay only on the way **back from Gozo**.
      - **Fast ferry:** from **Valletta** to **Mġarr** (Gozo) in about 45 minutes. Book online, especially in summer.
      - **Sicily:** a fast ferry from Valletta to Pozzallo takes about 1 hour 45 minutes. See [Trips to Sicily](/locations/sicily.html).

  - heading: Taxis, cars and other options
    text: |-
      - **Ride-hailing apps:** Bolt, Uber and eCabs are the easiest way to get a taxi. Prices are shown before you book.
      - **White taxis:** you can find them at the airport, the ports and in Valletta. Agree the price before you start, or use the fixed airport fares.
      - **Driving:** traffic is heavy at rush hour and parking is hard in busy areas. See [Driving License](/expat-info/driving-license.html).
      - **Cycling and scooters:** possible, but roads can be busy and narrow. Rental e-scooters are no longer available in Malta.

      Not sure which area to live in? Living near a main bus route, or in a central area like Sliema, Gżira, Msida or Valletta, makes life without a car much easier. Check rents with our [Rent Price Checker](/tools/rent-prices.html).
