  - heading: The job market in Malta
    text: |-
      Malta has one of the **lowest unemployment rates in Europe**, and many companies hire people from abroad. **English is an official language**, so most office jobs are in English. You do not need to speak Maltese for most jobs.

      **The main industries for expats:**

      - **Online gaming (iGaming):** marketing, customer support, IT, compliance and data roles. Many international companies have offices in Sliema, St Julian's and Gżira.
      - **Financial services:** banking, funds, insurance, accounting and audit.
      - **IT and tech:** software developers, data and cyber security are in high demand.
      - **Tourism and hospitality:** hotels, restaurants and bars, especially in summer.
      - **Healthcare:** nurses, carers and doctors.
      - **Aviation, manufacturing and pharmaceuticals:** engineers and technicians.
      - **Construction and transport:** builders, drivers and delivery work.

  - heading: Where to find jobs
    text: |-
      **Job websites:**

      - **Jobsplus** (jobsplus.gov.mt): the government employment agency, with a free job portal and training courses.
      - **LinkedIn:** the most used site for office, tech, finance and iGaming jobs in Malta. Set your location to Malta.
      - **KeepMePosted** and **JobsInMalta.com:** popular local job websites.
      - **Indeed:** many local and international listings.
      - **Company websites:** many big employers post jobs on their own career pages first.

      **Recruitment agencies:** agencies like **Konnekt**, **Castille Resources** and **Vacancy Centre** match people with jobs for free (the employer pays them). There are also agencies that focus only on iGaming or finance.

      **Other ways:**

      - **Networking:** Malta is small and many jobs are filled by word of mouth. Go to meet-ups and industry events, such as the big iGaming conferences.
      - **Expat groups** on Facebook often share job posts, but be careful with offers that ask you to pay money.

  - heading: CV and interview tips
    text: |-
      - **Keep your CV to 1 or 2 pages**, in English. Put your most recent job first.
      - **Show your right to work:** write your nationality, or say if you already have a Maltese permit.
      - **Say where you are:** "Based in Malta" or "Moving to Malta in March" helps employers.
      - A **photo is optional**. Many people add one, but it is not required.
      - **Interviews** are often online first, then in person. Dress smart-casual for most office jobs.
      - **Ask about the full package:** salary, bonuses, health insurance, working hours and remote work.

  - heading: Salaries and your contract
    text: |-
      - **Minimum wage (2026):** **€229.44 a week** for full-time workers aged 18 and over (about €994 a month before deductions).
      - Most salaries are quoted as a **gross yearly amount**. See your take-home pay with our free [Salary Calculator](/tools/salary-calculator.html).
      - Employees also get **statutory bonuses** four times a year (March, June, September and December).
      - A normal full-time week is **40 hours**. Most new jobs start with a **probation period** of up to 6 months.
      - Your employer must register your job with **Jobsplus** (the *engagement form*) and give you a written contract.

      Read more about your rights in [Employment in Malta](/expat-info/employment-in-malta.html).

  - heading: EU and non-EU citizens
    text: |-
      **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens** can live and work in Malta freely. You can come to Malta, look for a job, and start work once you are hired. Then apply for your [Residence Card](/work-business/residence-cards.html).

      **Non-EU citizens (third-country nationals)** need a **job offer first**:

      1. **Find an employer** who is willing to hire you from abroad.
      2. **The employer applies for your Single Permit** (work and residence permit) through Identità. You cannot usually change a tourist stay into a work permit.
      3. **Complete the pre-departure course and test** about life and work in Malta, if your permit requires it.
      4. **Hospitality and tourism jobs** also need a **Skills Pass**, a short test of your skills and English after you arrive.
      5. **Changing jobs** later means your new employer must apply for a new permit.

      Highly skilled workers with a high salary may use the faster **Key Employee Initiative (KEI)**. See [Permits and IDs](/expat-info/permits-ids.html) for details.

      > **Budget 2027:** the government announced **higher work permit fees** from 2027 (from €600 to €800, plus a yearly €400 contribution), and the end of free public transport for most non-EU residents. See [Public Transport](/quick-guides/public-transport.html).

  - heading: Job adverts for non-EU workers (including jobs with no special skills)
    text: |-
      Many non-EU workers in Malta work in **cleaning, care, hotels and restaurants, construction, security and warehouse jobs**. These jobs often do not need a degree or special skills. Here is where to find them safely:

      - **Jobsplus job portal** (jobsplus.gov.mt): since October 2025, employers who want to hire a non-EU worker **must first advertise the job on Jobsplus and on EURES**. So these job boards show many jobs that can lead to a **Single Permit**.
      - **EURES** (the European job portal): search with **Malta** as the country of work.
      - **Licensed recruitment agencies:** a licensed agency must show its **licence number** in every job advert. If there is no licence number, do not continue.
      - **Company career pages:** big hotel groups, cleaning companies, care homes and construction firms often list jobs on their own websites.

      **Important rules:**

      - **You should never pay for a job.** By Maltese law, employment agencies **cannot charge job seekers any fee**.
      - Only your **employer** or a **licensed agency** can apply for your Single Permit. Permit fees are paid on the official online system, **never into someone's personal bank account**.
      - From abroad, the job advert, pre-departure course and permit usually take **2 to 4 months** before you can start.
      - **Care jobs** (elderly, disability and health care) have a faster process and lower permit fee. **Hotel and restaurant jobs** need a **Skills Pass**.
      - **Lost your job while in Malta?** You usually have **30 days** to find a new employer (or **60 days** if you can show you can support yourself). Your new employer must apply for a new permit.

  - heading: Already working full-time? Rules for a second job
    text: |-
      If you are a non-EU citizen with a **Single Permit**, your permit allows you to work **only for the employer named on it**. Before you take any extra work, check these rules:

      **Part-time second job: allowed, with permission**

      - To work **part-time for a second employer**, you need a separate **Employment Licence from Jobsplus** for that part-time job.
      - The **second employer applies** to Jobsplus, using the checklist for *secondary employment*.
      - You **cannot start** the second job until the licence is approved. Working before approval is illegal for you **and** the employer.
      - Your main full-time job must continue. If it ends, your permit is affected.

      **Informal work ("cash in hand"): not allowed**

      Any work that is not declared to Jobsplus is **illegal work**, even for a few hours. This includes cash jobs, helping in a shop or restaurant, cleaning homes, and delivery or driving apps without the correct permit and licence. The risks are serious:

      - Your **permit can be cancelled** or **not renewed**, and you may have to **leave Malta**.
      - It can **harm future applications**, such as long-term residence or citizenship.
      - You pay **no social security**, so the work does not count for your **pension, sick pay or benefits**.
      - If you are **injured at work**, you may have **no insurance** and no protection.
      - The employer can be **fined**, and you have no contract to prove what you are owed.

      **Freelance or self-employed work** on the side is also **not allowed** on a normal Single Permit.

      > Not sure if a job is allowed? Ask **Jobsplus** (freephone **153**) or **Identità** before you start. It is free, and it can protect your permit.

  - heading: Avoid job scams
    text: |-
      - **Never pay to get a job.** Real employers and licensed recruitment agencies in Malta do not charge you for a job or a work permit.
      - Be careful with job offers that come only by WhatsApp or social media, with very high pay for simple work.
      - Check that the company is real: look it up on the Malta Business Registry or LinkedIn.
      - If you think you were cheated, contact **Jobsplus** or the **Department for Industrial and Employment Relations (DIER)**.
