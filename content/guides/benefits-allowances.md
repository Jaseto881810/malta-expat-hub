  - heading: Residency, Identity & SSN Registration
    text: |-
      The procedural initiation for any expat begins with the acquisition of a Social Security Number (SSN). The administrative landscape shifted in July 2025 toward an automated model intended to reduce bureaucratic friction for foreign workers.

      **Automated SSN Issuance**

      Under current regulations, employees holding a Maltese Residence Card ending in 'A' are not required to submit a separate application for an SSN. When an employer submits the Jobsplus Engagement Form to register a new hire, the system automatically transmits details to the Department of Social Security (DSS). If eligible, an SSN is generated and linked to the individual's identity record automatically.

      **Manual Application Procedures**

      EU nationals who choose not to apply for a Residence Card but are gainfully occupied must still use the manual application process through the servizz.gov portal or a physical hub.

      | Expatriate Status | Required Documentation |
      | --- | --- |
      | EU National (Employed) | Scanned copy of work contract or proof of self-occupation |
      | Non-EU National (Employed) | Signed work contract and Employment Licence (Work Permit) |
      | Non-EU Spouse of EU/Maltese | Marriage certificate and confirmation of freedom of movement rights |

      Once the SSN is issued, expats must update their banking details via the "mySocialSecurity" application, as Malta uses direct credit for benefit payments.

  - heading: Employment & National Insurance Contributions
    text: ''
    html: |-
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <p>Malta’s social security is financed through Class 1 (employed) and Class 2 (self-occupied) contributions.</p>
      <br/>
      <b>Class 1: The Employer-Employee Model</b>
      <br/>
      <p>For most expats, contributions involve a tripartite funding model: the employee pays 10%, the employer pays 10%, and the State contributes 10%. For 2026, the maximum weekly contribution cap for employees born on or after January 1, 1962, has risen to €55.93.</p>
      <br/>
      <b>Class 2: Self-Occupation and Passive Income</b>
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <ul class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-3">
      <li><b>Self-Occupied Persons:</b> Individuals earning from a trade or profession. Contributions are generally 15% of the previous year's net income</li>
      <li><b>Self-Employed Persons:</b> Those receiving passive income (rents, investments). These contributions cover pensions only, not short-term sickness or injury benefits.</li>
      </ul>
      </div>

  - heading: Family & Children’s Support
    text: ''
    html: |-
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <p>Malta’s family policy includes grants and allowances that scale with income and household size.</p>
      <br/>
      <b>Children’s Allowance and 2026 Updates</b>
      <br/>
      <p>Eligibility depends on permit status. EU nationals are generally eligible from arrival, while TCNs on standard permits typically qualify after six months of employment.</p>
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <p>Once you land at Malta International Airport (MLA), here are your immediate priorities:</p>
      <ul class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-3">
      <li><b>Income Assessment:</b> Since 2025, eligibility is calculated after deducting both social security contributions and income tax.</li>
      <li><b>2026 Rate Increases:</b> Families with income below €30,000 receive a €250 annual increase per child, with an additional €167 for those below €23,000.</li>
      </ul>
      </div>
      <br/>
      <b>Child Birth and Adoption Bonuses</b>
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <ul class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-3">
      <li><b>First Born:</b> €1,000.</li>
      <li><b>Second Born:</b> €1,500.</li>
      <li><b>Third and Subsequent Born:</b> €2,000.</li>
      </ul>

  - heading: Workplace Entitlements & Leave
    text: ''
    html: |-
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <p>Malta has modernized its leave regulations to align with the EU Work-Life Balance Directive.</p>
      <br/>
      <b>Maternity and Paternity Leave</b>
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <ul class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-3">
      <li><b>Maternity Leave:</b> 18 consecutive weeks. The first 14 weeks are paid by the employer at full wage; the final 4 weeks are paid by the government at the national minimum wage.</li>
      <li><b>Paternity Leave:</b> 10 working days of fully paid leave, funded by the employer.</li>
      </ul>
      </div>
      <b>New Statutory Leave (2026)</b>
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <ul class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-3">
      <li><b>Parental Bereavement Leave:</b> A new benefit allowing employers to request a refund for leave granted to parents following the loss of a minor child.</li>
      <li><b>Miscarriage Special Leave:</b> Available to the mother and her eligible spouse/partner, even if the mother is unemployed.</li>
      <li><b>Urgent Family Leave:</b> As of April 2025, this increased to 32 hours per year for full-time employees.</li>
      </ul>
      </div>
      </div>

  - heading: Healthcare & Medical Assistance
    text: ''
    html: |-
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <p>Expats paying social security are entitled to free state healthcare. Short-term illness is managed via the "Blue Medical Certificate."</p>
      <br/>
      <b>Sickness Benefits</b>
      <br/>
      <p>The employer pays for the first three days of illness. Government benefits commence on the fourth day.</p>
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <ul class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-3">
      <li><b>2026 Rates:</b> €17.21 per day for single persons and €25.81 per day for married persons.</li>
      <li><b>Long-Term Sickness:</b> If illness persists for 156 continuous days, the claim converts to Increased Sickness Benefit (ISB), paid at a rate similar to an invalidity pension.</li>
      </ul>
      </div>
      <br/>
      <b>Free Medical Aid (The Pink Form)</b>
      <br/>
      <p>The Pink Form provides free certain medical services and prescriptions to those with financial constraints or chronic illnesses.</p>
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <ul class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-3">
      <li><b>2026 Eligibility Update:</b> Residents aged 65 or over who receive the Supplementary Allowance are now automatically eligible for the Pink Form without a separate means test.</li>
      </ul>
      </div>
      </div>

      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <b>Additional Cost of Living Adjustment (ACLA)</b>
      <p>The ACLA is a non-taxable payment for low and middle-income households. In 2026, payments range from €200 to €1,500 per year, depending on family composition.</p>
      <br/>
      <b>Unemployment Support</b>
      <br/>
      <div class="text-slate-600 space-y-4 leading-relaxed">
      <ul class="list-decimal pl-5 space-y-3">
      <li><b>EU Nationals:</b> May claim benefit if they have a Maltese work record. They can "export" these benefits for up to three months while seeking work in another EU country.</li>
      <li><b>Third-Country Nationals:</b> Generally ineligible for unemployment benefits, as residence permits are typically tied to a specific employer.</li>
      </ul>

  - heading: Retirement & Pension Portability
    text: |-
      The statutory retirement age is gradually increasing to 65. In 2026, all state pensions will receive a mandatory increase of €10 per week.

      **Retirement Age by Birth Year**

      | Birth Year | Statutory Retirement Age | Early Retirement (Age 61) |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | 1952 - 1955 | 62 years old | 35 years of contributions |
      | 1959 - 1961 | 64 years old | 35 years of contributions |
      | 1962 - 1968 | 65 years old | 40 years of contributions |
      | 1976 onwards | 65 years old | 42 years of contributions |

      **Pension Portability**

      Under EU coordination rules, contributions made in Malta are aggregated with those made in other member states. When an expat retires, Malta pays a pro-rata pension based on the years worked on the island.
