howItWorks: |-
  1. **Enter your yearly income.** Use your chargeable income: your gross pay, minus any allowed deductions.
  2. **Choose if you are a resident.** Most people who live and work in Malta are tax residents. Non-residents use different rates.
  3. **Choose your tax status.** Your status (single, married or parent) decides the size of each tax band.
  4. **Add other income (optional).** Part-time work can be taxed at a flat 10% (up to €10,000 a year). Rental income can be taxed at a flat 15% on the gross rent.
  5. **See your tax.** The table shows how much of your income falls in each band, and the tax for each band.

  Malta has progressive tax: each rate only applies to the part of your income inside that band. A pay rise never makes your net income go down.
faqs:
  - question: What are the income tax rates in Malta for 2026?
    answer: For a single person, the first €12,000 is tax-free. Income from €12,001 to €16,000 is taxed at 15%, from €16,001 to €60,000 at 25%, and above €60,000 at 35%. Married and parent rates have bigger tax-free bands.
  - question: What changed in 2026?
    answer: From 2026 there are four new rate tables for families. Married couples and parents with one child, or with two or more children, get larger tax-free and lower bands. You need to meet the conditions and give your employer a new FS4 form.
  - question: Who is a tax resident in Malta?
    answer: Usually, you are a tax resident if you live in Malta and plan to stay, or if you spend more than 183 days a year in Malta. If you are not sure, ask a tax adviser or the Malta Tax and Customs Administration (MTCA).
  - question: What is the 10% part-time tax rate?
    answer: Income from a part-time job can be taxed at a flat 10%, on up to €10,000 a year. You must also have a full-time job, or meet other conditions. You can choose normal rates instead if this is better for you.
  - question: How is rental income taxed?
    answer: If you rent out property in Malta, you can choose to pay a flat 15% tax on the gross rent. Otherwise the rent is added to your other income and taxed at normal rates.
  - question: When do I file my tax return?
    answer: Employees usually pay tax every month through their payslip (the FSS system). Most employees with simple income do not need to file a return. If you have other income, the tax return is normally due by 30 June of the following year.
