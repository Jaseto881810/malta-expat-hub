Moving to a new country is exciting, but the first weeks can feel like a lot. Here are seven things that will make your start in Malta easier.

### 1. Download the apps before you land

**Bolt** and **Uber** are the easiest way to get around, and a ride from the airport to Sliema usually costs between €12 and €15. For food and groceries, use **Wolt** or **Bolt Food**. See our [Getting Started](/getting-started.html) guide for the full list.

### 2. Get a personalised bus card

With a personalised **Tallinja** card, buses are free for residents. Without one, a single trip costs €2. Buses are not always on time, so leave early for important appointments.

### 3. Start your paperwork early

Your residence card, work permit and tax number all take time. Book appointments as soon as you can, and keep copies of every document. Our [Permits and IDs](/expat-info/permits-ids.html) guide explains each step.

### 4. Know how to see a doctor

Most GPs in Malta hold clinic hours inside local pharmacies. You usually walk in without an appointment and pay about **€15–25**. In an emergency, call **112**. More in our [Healthcare](/expat-info/healthcare.html) guide.

### 5. Take your time finding a flat

Rent is high in Sliema, St Julian's and Swieqi. Towns like Mosta and Żebbuġ often cost less. Visit a flat before you pay a deposit, and always get a registered contract. Read [Renting Properties](/accommodation/renting.html) first.

### 6. Prepare for the summer heat

From June to September, days reach 30–35°C and nights stay warm. Air conditioning is a must, and humidity can be high all year. Check our weather notes on the [home page](/index.html).

### 7. Say hello to your neighbours

The Maltese are friendly and helpful once you get to know them. Learn a few words of Maltese, like *grazzi* (thank you), and join a local festa in summer. See [Local Etiquette](/basic-info/etiquette.html) and [Annual Festivals](/lifestyle/festivals.html).
